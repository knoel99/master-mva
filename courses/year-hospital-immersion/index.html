This is a framing page, not a course. The module pairs a student with a physician on a real clinical question and real-life care data.

According to master-mva.com, it is neither an internship nor a research project. It is a first contact with medical questions in the field, with the skills needed to interpret them mathematically, and with real-world care data. The goal is to propose an answer to the physician’s question with the tools of the master.

What the module asks

  • Understand the need. Talk with the physicians, with the people who collected the data and with other care staff, until the clinical question is stated precisely enough to be answered.
  • Translate it into a statistical or learning problem. What is the outcome, what are the covariates, what population does the data base represent, what would change a clinical decision?
  • Propose a possible solution from the data base entrusted to the student, with its limits stated plainly.

How to prepare

The Santé-track pages of these notes cover most of the tools such a project needs:

  • study design, survival analysis and confounding: Biostatistics;
  • estimation, EM and simulation methods: Computational Statistics;
  • images: Medical Image Analysis and Deep Learning for Medical Imaging;
  • brain signals: Functional Brain Imaging and Brain–Computer Interfaces;
  • shapes and anatomy: Geometric Data Analysis and Geometry and Shape Spaces.

Health data is personal data: anonymisation, access rules and the GDPR apply from the first day.

Deliverables and assessment

  • An oral defence of 15 minutes followed by 10 minutes of questions, before the end of March, organised at the convenience of the student and the people involved.
  • The referent physician, the other people the student worked with, and an MVA instructor attend.

These are the official rules from master-mva.com; this page adds no assessment of its own.
